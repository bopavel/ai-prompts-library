# First Draft Protector

**Best For**: 
- Fiction writers struggling to finish first drafts
- Anyone who gets stuck in endless revision cycles
- Writers with perfectionist tendencies
- Creating initial content before refinement (blog posts, reports, scripts)

---

## Usage Examples

### Example 1: Blog Post - Overcoming Perfectionist Attitude

**Writer**: "I'm writing a blog post about time management. I have this intro but I hate it: 'Time management is important for productivity.' That's so boring and generic!"

**AI**: "First drafts are allowed to be boring! That's 7 words on paper. What's your second point?"

**Writer**: "I want to talk about the myth that busy equals productive. But should this be the second paragraph or should I start with a story first?"

**AI**: "Pick one now, change it later if needed. What are you going with?"

**Writer**: "Okay, story first. Last week I worked 60 hours and accomplished nothing meaningful. Is 'accomplished nothing meaningful' too vague?"

**AI**: "It's fine for now! 19 words total. What specific thing didn't you accomplish?"

**Writer**: "I didn't finish the client proposal that actually mattered. I spent the whole week in useless meetings and answering emails. God, this is such bad writing."

**AI**: "Shitty first drafts are the path to good final drafts. You're at 40 words. What's your main point about this story?"

---

### Example 2: Research Paper - Breaking Through Academic Block

**Writer**: "I'm writing the introduction to my thesis. I wrote: 'This paper examines the relationship between social media usage and anxiety in college students.' But that's such a weak thesis statement."

**AI**: "That's editing thinking. Right now, we're drafting. You have a starting point, keep building. What's the next sentence?"

**Writer**: "Previous research has shown... actually, I should probably cite specific studies here."

**AI**: "Write [CITE LATER] and continue. What has previous research shown?"

**Writer**: "Previous research has shown [CITE LATER] that social media correlates with anxiety, but the mechanism isn't clear. However, I really need to define 'social media' more precisely first"

**AI**: "You can add definitions later. You've got 35 words. What's the gap your research fills?"

**Writer**: "My research looks at the specific types of interactions, not just usage time. Active posting versus passive scrolling show different patterns..."

**AI**: "Keep going! You're in flow now. What patterns do they show?"

---

### Example 3: Email/Communication - Quick Draft Mode

**Writer**: "I need to write a difficult email to my team about project delays. Here's what I have: 'Hi team, I need to discuss our timeline.' That sounds too harsh."

**AI**: "First draft mode: get your thoughts out, polish tone later. What do you need to tell them?"

**Writer**: "The client changed requirements mid-sprint, so we're two weeks behind. I need everyone to work some extra hours. Wait, 'work extra hours' sounds demanding"

**AI**: "Write it! Fix tone later. What else needs to be in this email?"

**Writer**: "I need to acknowledge this is frustrating and thank them for their flexibility. Also mention we're adjusting the timeline. Is this email getting too long?"

**AI**: "That's an editing concern. Draft complete? Yes or no?"

**Writer**: "Yes, I think I have all the points."

**AI**: "Draft complete! 75 words. Ready to switch to edit mode for tone and polish?"
