# Pre-Mortem Facilitator

**Best For**: 
- New product launches
- Major organizational changes
- Strategic initiatives with high stakes
- Cross-functional projects with dependencies
- Decisions with significant downside risk

---

## Customization Guide

### Adjust Time Horizon

**For Fast-Moving Projects**:
```
Modify PHASE 1:
"It's 3 months from now..." (shorter timeline for faster feedback)
```

**For Long-Term Initiatives**:
```
"It's 2 years from now..." (captures strategic shifts, market changes)
```

---

### Adapt Formality Level

**For Executive Teams**:
```
Add:
"Frame as 'scenario planning for downside cases.' Use business language.
Focus on strategic and reputational risks."
```

**For Startup Teams**:
```
"Keep it casual and fast-paced. Use 'what could kill us?' framing.
Focus on execution and market risks."
```

---

### Domain-Specific Focus

**For Tech Projects**:
```
Add to PHASE 2 categories:
- Technical debt failures (shortcuts came back to haunt us)
- Security failures (we got breached/hacked)
- Scalability failures (it worked at small scale, broke at large scale)
```

**For Marketing Campaigns**:
```
Add categories:
- Brand damage failures (the campaign backfired publicly)
- Targeting failures (reached wrong audience)
- Timing failures (message was tone-deaf given current events)
```

---

### Combine with Planning

**After Pre-Mortem**:
```
"Now let's flip it. Assume wild success. What went right? What enabled it?
Use those insights to reinforce success factors."
```

## Limitations & Edge Cases

### Won't Work Well For

**Very Low-Risk Decisions**:
- **Why**: Overhead isn't worth it for reversible, low-stakes choices
- **Alternative**: Quick gut check: "What's the worst case?" and move on

**Teams Already Paralyzed by Risk**:
- **Why**: Can amplify existing anxiety and prevent action
- **Alternative**: Use success-focused planning instead; come back to pre-mortem later

**When Project Must Happen Regardless**:
- **Why**: If leadership has decided "we're doing this no matter what," pre-mortem feels fake
- **Alternative**: Frame as "risk mitigation planning" not "should we do this?"

**Post-Launch Situations**:
- **Why**: Already happened; use retrospective instead
- **Alternative**: "What would we pre-mortem if we could do it again?"

---

### Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Becoming Actually Pessimistic**
- **Symptom**: Team loses enthusiasm and momentum for the project
- **Solution**: Time-box strictly; end with action plan and excitement about prevention

**Pitfall 2: Blame Culture Emerges**
- **Symptom**: People use pre-mortem to criticize others' past work
- **Solution**: Reinforce "this is about the future, not the past" and "systemic issues, not people"

**Pitfall 3: Analysis Paralysis**
- **Symptom**: Team identifies 50 risks and doesn't know where to start
- **Solution**: Force prioritization - top 5-7 only. Rest go in "monitor" category.

**Pitfall 4: No Follow-Through**
- **Symptom**: Great pre-mortem, but actions never happen
- **Solution**: Assign owners and deadlines IN the meeting; schedule follow-up

---

### Edge Cases

**Edge Case 1: Remote/Async Teams**
- **How to adapt**: Use collaborative doc; people write failure scenarios independently first, then discuss
- **Recommendation**: Works well, independent thinking often surfaces more ideas

**Edge Case 2: Solo Decision-Maker**
- **How to adapt**: Do it yourself; imagining failure still works individually
- **Recommendation**: Less psychological safety benefit, but still valuable for uncovering blind spots

**Edge Case 3: Highly Innovative/Uncertain Projects**
- **How to adapt**: Many unknowns are legitimate; focus on "what failure modes are preventable?"
- **Recommendation**: Pre-mortem helps distinguish acceptable risk from avoidable mistakes

**Edge Case 4: Regulated Industries**
- **How to adapt**: Add "compliance failure" as a specific category
- **Recommendation**: Especially valuable, failure to predict regulatory issues is common and costly

## Advanced Usage

### Recurring Pre-Mortems
```
"Do mini pre-mortems at each major milestone:
Month 3: Assume we fail in next 3 months. Why?
Month 6: Assume we fail to hit launch. Why?
Post-launch: Assume we fail to hit targets. Why?"
```

---

### Stakeholder Pre-Mortem
```
"Do separate pre-mortems from different stakeholder perspectives:
- From engineering's POV: technical failures
- From marketing's POV: market failures  
- From customers' POV: product failures"
```

---

### Competitive Pre-Mortem
```
"Assume our competitor launched first and crushed us. What did they do that we failed to do?"
```
