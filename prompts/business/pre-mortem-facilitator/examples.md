# Pre-Mortem Facilitator

**Best For**: 
- New product launches
- Major organizational changes
- Strategic initiatives with high stakes
- Cross-functional projects with dependencies
- Decisions with significant downside risk

---

## Usage Examples

### Example 1: Product Launch Pre-Mortem

**Facilitator**: "It's 6 months from now. Our new mobile app launched and completely flopped. It's been pulled from the app store. The CEO is asking how we spent $2M on this. What happened?"

**Team Member 1**: "Users hated the onboarding. Churn was 90% after the first screen."

**Facilitator**: "Good. Say more, what specifically about onboarding?"

**Team Member 1**: "We asked for too many permissions upfront, required account creation before showing any value, and the tutorial was 12 screens long."

**Facilitator**: "Perfect. That's concrete. What else?"

**Team Member 2**: "We launched with critical bugs. People couldn't complete sign-up on Android devices."

**Facilitator**: "Why did we ship with those bugs?"

**Team Member 2**: "We skipped beta testing because we were behind schedule. Only tested on our team's devices."

**Team Member 3**: "Actually, I think the bigger failure was we built features nobody wanted. We assumed people would use it like the web version, but mobile user needs are completely different."

**Facilitator**: "Interesting: assumption failure. What assumption specifically?"

**Team Member 3**: "That mobile users want all the same features. Really they want 2-3 quick actions, not full functionality."

**Facilitator**: "Great. Let's catalog these: Onboarding friction, Android bugs from skipped testing, feature-market mismatch. On a scale of 1-10, how likely is the onboarding issue?"

**Team Member 1**: "Honestly? 8. We haven't tested it with real users yet."

**Facilitator**: "And impact if it happens?"

**Team Member 1**: "9. If people churn immediately, we're dead."

**Facilitator**: "That's high-likelihood, high-impact. What's the early warning signal?"

**Team Member 1**: "We could run the onboarding flow with 20 people before launch. If >50% abandon, we know it's a problem."

**Facilitator**: "Perfect. What's the preventive action?"

**Team Member 1**: "User testing next week with revised onboarding. I'll own that."

**Facilitator**: "Great. Let's document that and move to the Android bug scenario..."

### Example 2: Strategic Partnership Pre-Mortem

**Facilitator**: "It's 18 months out. The partnership with [Company X] that we spent 6 months negotiating has blown up acrimoniously. Why?"

**BD Lead**: "They felt we didn't deliver on our promises. Specifically, we said we'd integrate their API within 3 months, but it took 9."

**Facilitator**: "Why did it take 9 months?"

**Engineering Lead**: "Their API documentation was incomplete, and their support was unresponsive. But also, honestly, we underestimated complexity."

**BD Lead**: "And by month 6, they were already frustrated and stopped prioritizing our requests."

**Facilitator**: "What else?"

**Product Manager**: "We had different definitions of success. We thought success was technical integration. They thought success was revenue from joint customers within 6 months."

**Facilitator**: "So we never aligned on success metrics. What else?"

**BD Lead**: "Their key champion left the company 4 months in. New person had no context and questioned the whole partnership."

**Facilitator**: "Personnel turnover risk. Anything else?"

**Legal**: "We didn't have clear IP ownership boundaries. When we built something cool together, we fought about who owns it."

**Facilitator**: "Okay, let's rate these. Integration delays - likelihood?"

**Engineering Lead**: "6. We haven't actually tested their API yet."

**Facilitator**: "Impact?"

**BD Lead**: "9. If we miss deadlines, trust evaporates fast."

**Facilitator**: "What's the preventive action?"

**Engineering Lead**: "API proof of concept this week. Four hours of engineering time to test integration before signing."

**Facilitator**: "Who owns it?"

**Engineering Lead**: "I do. I'll have results by Friday."

**Facilitator**: "Good. What about different definitions of success?"

**Product Manager**: "We need a joint success dashboard agreed upon before signing. Both parties define what good looks like with specific metrics."

**Facilitator**: "Who drives that?"

**Product Manager**: "I'll draft it, then we review with them in our next meeting."

**Facilitator**: "Excellent. Now, the personnel turnover. We can't prevent their champion from leaving. What can we do?"

**BD Lead**: "Build relationships with multiple people on their side, not just one champion. Also get executive sponsorship on both sides."

**Facilitator**: "How do we ensure that happens?"

**BD Lead**: "Partnership kickoff meeting with execs from both companies. Make it a recurring quarterly review."

**Facilitator**: "Document that as a requirement in the partnership agreement?"

**BD Lead**: "Yes."

### Example 3: Short Pre-Mortem for Quick Decision

**Facilitator**: "Quick pre-mortem: We're deciding whether to hire this senior candidate. It's one year later, and it was a disastrous hire. Why?"

**Hiring Manager**: "They couldn't work with the existing team. Personality clash."

**Facilitator**: "Signals we could check now?"

**Hiring Manager**: "Have them work on a mini-project with the team for a day before deciding."

**Team Lead**: "Or they were great individually but couldn't mentor juniors, which we need."

**Facilitator**: "How can we test that?"

**Team Lead**: "Ask them to explain a complex technical concept to me as if I'm a junior dev. See their teaching style."

**HR**: "Or they couldn't adapt to our pace. They came from a big corp, we're startup speed."

**Facilitator**: "What question surfaces that?"

**HR**: "Ask about their worst experience with ambiguity and changing priorities. Do they thrive or struggle?"

**Facilitator**: "Great. Three tests to run: team chemistry day, teaching ability demo, ambiguity tolerance questions. Let's do all three before deciding."
